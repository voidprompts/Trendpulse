How TrendPulse works

TrendPulse combines two different kinds of trend data. Understanding the difference is what makes the output useful rather than generic.

⚡

Micro trends — live, on demand

When you type a keyword, your browser queries a public encyclopedia search index for the articles most related to it, then pulls the actual pageview counts for each one over your chosen window. Topics are ranked by genuine traffic volume, and a velocity figure compares recent days against earlier ones so you can see what is accelerating rather than merely large. Every request is made directly from your browser, so there is no server to rate-limit or ban.

🌍

Macro trends — baked in daily

Separately, a scheduled Python job reads the global trending-search feed once every 24 hours and writes the nine biggest topics straight into this page as static HTML. Because that content is pre-rendered rather than loaded by JavaScript, search engines index it immediately — which is also why the board above loads instantly with no spinner.

Choosing tags that actually help

Trending tags work because they attach your post to a conversation that already has momentum. The catch is that momentum decays fast — a topic peaking on Tuesday morning is often cold by Wednesday night. That is why the generator lets you narrow the window: a 48-hour view surfaces what is spiking right now, while a 90-day view surfaces durable interest that will still be searched next month.

Read the velocity column rather than just the view count. A topic with two million views and flat velocity is saturated; creators have already covered it from every angle. A topic with eighty thousand views and sharply rising velocity is where you can still be early, and being early is most of the advantage.

Mix breadth with specificity. Broad amplifiers give reach but enormous competition, while the topic-specific tags pulled from live data give a smaller, far more relevant audience. Using both in one post generally outperforms either alone — which is why the generator returns a blend rather than a single category.

Relevance still beats volume. Platforms increasingly down-rank posts whose tags do not match the content, so only keep a suggestion you can genuinely tie to your subject. Delete the rest; a tight set of eight honest tags will outperform thirty borrowed ones. On YouTube in particular, tags are a minor ranking signal compared with your title and thumbnail — treat them as disambiguation, not as a growth strategy on their own.

Frequently asked questions

Where does the live data come from?

From the public Wikimedia search and pageviews APIs. Wikipedia is one of the largest free sources of global attention data: when a subject spikes in the news, its article traffic spikes with it, usually within hours. The generator reads those figures directly in your browser and converts the leading topics into tag strings.

Why do the numbers stop a day or two ago?

Pageview statistics are published in batches and typically lag real time by roughly 24 to 48 hours. The generator deliberately ends its query before that boundary so a window never contains empty days that would distort the ranking.

What happens if the API is throttled or offline?

The generator degrades gracefully. If the live request fails, times out, or returns nothing usable, it falls back to a structured set of high-volume evergreen fields built from your keyword and the platform you selected, and clearly labels the output as offline mode. You always get a usable string.

How are YouTube tags different?

YouTube Studio expects a comma-separated list of natural phrases rather than hashtags, so selecting YouTube returns lowercase multi-word phrases with no hash symbols, trimmed to stay within the 500-character field limit. Every other platform returns hash-prefixed tags.

Does TrendPulse collect my personal data?

The site has no accounts, no database and no contact forms, and your keywords are never sent to us — they go directly from your browser to the public data API. Third-party advertising partners may set cookies, which is explained on our Privacy Policy page.
